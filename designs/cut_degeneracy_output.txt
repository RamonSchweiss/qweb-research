
How many cuts are equally good, and what it costs to change between them.

Belongs to designs/design_cut_degeneracy_EN.md, version 1, registered
4 November 2026 before this run. H1, H2 and H3 are measured as registered.
The registered OBJECTIVE is not measured, and section 4 of this printout says
why: it is vacuous as written, which is a defect in the registration and not a
result. Its replacement needs its own dated registration first.

The published measurement says the gate-optimal cut does not move: in 21 of 21
problem graphs the exact minimum gate cut is the same at the first layer and at
the last. That is a statement about the cut COST. This asks how many partitions
reach that cost, and what a scheduler pays to change between them.

Currency : one ebit per qubit moved between processors, as in staleness_cost.py.
           Changing from P to Q moves the qubits whose side differs, so the
           migration cost is the Hamming distance of the membership vectors,
           minimised over which processor holds which side. Both partitions
           being balanced, that distance is even and at least two for P != Q.

Ground
truth    : exact enumeration of every balanced bipartition, to n = 20
           (92,378 partitions). No heuristic result is ever used as the
           optimum, which is an invalidating condition in section 5 of the note.

Needs    : numpy, scipy, partition_topology.
Runtime  : a few minutes, dominated by the n = 20 enumerations.

2 - H2: is the realised cost below the diameter?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Registered: the distance between the optimal partitions two
  independent randomised runs return is smaller than the diameter,
  because refinement from a random start does not sample the
  optimal set uniformly. Twenty starts per graph, as registered.

  'spread over seeds' is the resolution limit declared in section 6:

  unresolved, including where it agrees with a hypothesis.

4 - The registered objective is vacuous, and that is a defect
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Section 3 of the note registered this objective: an
  incumbent-preferring tie-break - among all exactly optimal
  partitions, return the one nearest the current assignment -
  reduces the realised migration cost to zero without giving up a
  crossing gate.

  It does, and it cannot do otherwise. If the incumbent is itself
  optimal, the nearest optimal partition to it IS it, at distance
  zero. No run can come out any other way. The same holds for a
  refinement warm-started at the incumbent: pairwise swapping only
  accepts strict improvements, so from an optimal partition it makes
  no move at all.

  This is an objective whose outcome was fixed before the
  measurement, which is the failure the design notes exist to
  prevent. It is reported as a WITHDRAWAL of the objective, not as
  a confirmation of it, and the defect is mine and in the
  registration rather than in the data.

  The non-vacuous question sits one step away: the interesting case
  is an incumbent that is NOT optimal, which the scoping run showed
  happens in 1 to 13 of 21 graphs depending on the heuristic. Then
  a warm-started refinement may or may not reach the optimum, and
  may or may not cost less to get there than a cold restart does.
  That has a real answer and is NOT measured here: it needs its own
  dated registration first, and measuring it in the same run that
  found the defect would be choosing the question after seeing the
  data.
