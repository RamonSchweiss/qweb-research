Could ANY optimiser rescue the control's evaluability?

The E2 probe showed that Adam does not change the SHAPE of the control's
start distribution -- every cell still has starts either at the solution or
far below it, with the 'between' column zero in seven of eight cells -- but
it does change the MIXING FRACTION, the share of starts that reach the
solution. Adam at its best put 13 of 15 starts in the upper mode where
gradient descent put 9 of 15.

So the question the probe cannot answer by itself: how high would the mixing
fraction have to go before the registered noise floor falls under the 0.05
limit of section 6? If that number is unreachable, then no step rule and no
step size rescues the control, the choice between E1 and E3 does not wait on
any further optimiser work, and a hypothetical amendment of section 3 would
buy nothing even if one were willing to make it.

This is a MODEL, not a measurement, and the distinction matters. The two mode
locations are taken from the main run's stored scores at D2, n = 8. The
mixing fraction is then swept, and for each value the REGISTERED machinery --
verdict.arm_spread, unchanged -- is applied to simulated draws of the same
shape as the real ones: 20 draws, 5 starts each. What is simulated is only
which mode each start falls into.

==========================================================================
THE TWO MODES, from the stored run
==========================================================================
  100 starts in 20 draws
  upper mode   74 starts   mean 1.0000   sd 0.0000
  lower mode   26 starts   mean 0.5115   sd 0.0275
  between       0 starts
  observed mixing fraction p = 0.7400
  gap between the modes       0.4885

==========================================================================
THE SWEEP  (registered verdict.arm_spread, 20 draws x 5 starts)
==========================================================================
  Each start lands in the upper mode with probability p, and its score
  is resampled from that mode's observed scores. 400 repetitions per p,
  the median floor reported. Section 6's limit is 0.05.

  The floor first falls under 0.05 at p = 0.95.

  First, the model checks out: at the observed p it reproduces the
  measured floor of 0.1814 to within three thousandths, so the
  two-point picture is not an idealisation that flatters the answer.

  Read the crossing against what the probe measured. Gradient descent
  at the registered step size put 9 of 15 starts in the upper mode;
  Adam at its best put 13 of 15, which is p = 0.867 and a floor of
  about 0.12 -- still more than twice the limit. The floor is driven by
  the within-draw spread, which falls as sqrt(p(1-p)) and is nearly
  flat across the whole range the optimisers actually reach.

  So a better optimiser does not rescue the control. It would have to
  carry the mixing fraction from 0.87 past 0.95, and the probe's own
  spread of the per-draw scores -- 1.000, 0.912, 0.910 at Adam's best
  step size -- shows draws where it does not even reach the solution
  in every start. The choice between E1 and E3 therefore does not wait
  on any further optimiser work.
