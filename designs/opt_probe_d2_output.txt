The optimiser grid of A3.4, run on the CONTROL D2 instead of on D1.

WHY THIS IS NOT A DESIGN CHANGE.  opt_probe.py, 15 November, fixed the step
rule and the step size for the whole study, and its own first line says what
it ran on: "the optimiser grid on the PRIMARY D1". Two different things were
settled there, and only one of them was settled by measurement.

  THE RULE was settled by reading section 3 narrowly -- plain gradient
  descent -- and that is a textual commitment about what the design says. No
  measurement on any dataset can overturn it, and none is asked to here.
  Adam was carried in the grid for one declared reason: "the addendum owes a
  figure for what the narrow reading costs in training score rather than an
  assurance that it costs little." That figure exists for D1 only. The
  addendum owes it for the control too, and this is it.

  THE STEP SIZE, 0.30, was selected by the criterion of A3.4 as amended --
  the pair reaching the higher TRAINING score at the measured run length --
  over the grid 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, at n = 8, over three draws, on D1. It
  was then used on D2 without ever being selected there. Whether 0.30 is also
  what the registered criterion picks for gradient descent on the control has
  never been measured. That is a gap in the execution of the design, not a
  change to it, and it is the gap this closes.

SELECTION AND DIAGNOSIS ARE KEPT APART.  The criterion is blind to the test
score and stays blind here: the winner is chosen on the training score and on
nothing else. The test-score distribution is printed underneath, for every
cell, because the question that sent me here -- whether the control's
bimodality is R1 version 2 itself or the optimiser -- is a question about test
scores. It is reported. It does not select.

So the two readings this can produce, both declared before the run:

  The bimodality survives every cell. Then it is a property of R1 version 2
  under this circuit and this cost, the step rule is exonerated, and the
  control is simply not solvable reliably -- decision E1 or E3.

  Some cell removes it. Then the step size selected on D1 was the wrong one
  for the control, by the design's own criterion, and that is a fourth
  instrument defect of the same family as the other three. It would be
  repaired by re-selecting the step size per dataset under A3.4, which is the
  criterion already registered. A switch to ADAM would still not follow: the
  rule is the narrow reading of section 3, and a training score cannot amend
  a reading. Adam's column stays a figure for what the narrow reading costs.

Run length is 300 steps, not the study's 500, because 300 is what A3.4's
rule-and-step-size selection was executed at on D1 and this has to be the
same procedure to be comparable. STEPS = 500 was fixed separately, measured
at n = 12, and is untouched by this.
