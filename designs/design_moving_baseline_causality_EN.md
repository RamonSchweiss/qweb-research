# Design note: does a quantum claim cause the classical improvement?

**Date:** 8 November 2026. Version 1, registered before any searching.

**What is under test.** A claim I made publicly this morning, in a comment on
LinkedIn:

> "better than the best classical methods" is a moving target, and a quantum
> paper is one of the things that moves it.

The second half is a **causal** assertion. The merker it came from
(`bewegliches-ziel`) records it as unverified, and it is: what I actually know
is a sequence of events, not a mechanism. Recommendation algorithms were
dequantised after a quantum version existed. Supremacy claims were narrowed by
tensor-network work published afterwards. **Afterwards is not because of.**

**Why this is registered rather than just looked up.** The failure mode here is
specific and seductive: every case I can think of has the right chronology, and
chronology will read as causation unless the evidence standard is fixed first.
That is the same error as reading an agreement as structure, and this programme
has been caught by it twice.

---

## 1 The evidence standard, fixed before searching

For each case examined, the causal link is classified as one of:

**STATED.** The authors of the *classical* work say, in their own paper, that
the quantum result motivated it — as motivation, as the problem they set out to
match, or as the benchmark they aimed at. A citation alone is not enough; a
citation is what one does for any related work. The claim must be about why the
work was undertaken.

**IMPLIED.** The classical work is framed throughout as a response — it uses the
quantum result's problem instance, its parameters, or its benchmark, and would
not have the shape it has without it — but no motivational statement is made.

**CHRONOLOGICAL ONLY.** The classical work came later and addresses the same
problem, with nothing further connecting them.

**INDEPENDENT.** The classical line of work predates the quantum claim or
developed separately.

**The claim as I stated it requires STATED or IMPLIED cases.** Chronological-only
cases do not support it, however many of them there are, and listing many would
be the trap rather than the evidence.

## 2 The cases to examine, named now

1. **Dequantisation of recommendation systems.** Quantum algorithm first, then a
   classical algorithm with comparable scaling. The best-known instance, and the
   one most often told as a causal story.
2. **Classical simulation of random-circuit sampling claims.** Tensor-network
   and related methods that narrowed or matched announced supremacy results.
3. **Quantum annealing advantage claims** matched by simulated annealing or
   parallel tempering.

No case is added after results are seen. If a fourth presents itself it is
recorded as a late discovery and the report says whether it was used.

## 3 Declared degenerate subset

- Cases where the classical method already existed and was merely *applied* to
  the quantum paper's instance. The target did not move; someone pointed at
  where it already was.
- Cases where the classical improvement is a straightforward engineering
  follow-up that the classical field would plainly have reached anyway.

Both are excluded by definition, before searching.

## 4 Invalidating condition

**Reading a motivational framing into a paper that does not state one.** The
STATED category requires a statement I can quote. If I find myself
reconstructing an author's reasons from the shape of their work, that is the
IMPLIED category at best, and the report says so rather than upgrading it.

## 5 Result-independent stopping condition

The three named cases, each examined for a statement of motivation in the
classical work itself. The run ends when all three are classified, whatever the
classifications are. No further cases are sought because the first three came
out weakly.

## 6 What each outcome means, decided now

**Two or more STATED.** The claim stands as I made it, and the article can use
the causal form with the quotations as its evidence.

**One STATED, or only IMPLIED.** The claim must be weakened to its defensible
version: the target moves, and quantum claims are among the things it moves
*around*, without asserting that they caused the movement. The comment I posted
this morning then needs a correction, and the article is written in the weaker
form — which is still worth writing.

**Chronological only.** The causal claim fails. The article keeps its first
half — that the comparison target is not stationary and that a result must be
stated so it can be re-run — and drops the second entirely. The posted comment
needs a correction either way.

**Mixed with a degenerate majority.** Reported as such. If most of what looks
like evidence falls into section 3, that is the finding.

## 7 What this does not establish

Whether the phenomenon is common. Three named cases cannot measure prevalence,
and the report will not be written as though they could. It establishes only
what the documentary record says about these three.
